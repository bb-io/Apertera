A day in the heart of the Loire: castles and vineyards

Morning: Chateau de Chambord Start your day with a visit to the legendary Chateau de Chambord, a masterpiece of French Renaissance architecture. This huge castle with 400 rooms, the legendary double spiral staircase and wooded grounds was created for King Francis I as a farming lodge. Wander the grand halls and rooftop terraces, which offer stunning views of the Loire countryside. Arrive early in the afternoon with a little change and well to hate the quiet and nice grandeur of the chateau before the crowds arrive.

Afternoon: Lunch in Blois Just a short drive away is the town of Blois, where you can experience the typical charm of the Loire Valley. Explore the medieval center, its winding streets and the palatial Château de Blois, known for its eclectic mix of architectural styles. Stop for lunch at a local bistro where you can enjoy Loire specialties such as goat cheese tartine or rillettes. Snack on a glass of crisp Sancerre wine, grown just a few kilometers away.

Evening: Amboise and the legacy of Leonardo da Vinci Head to the Chateau of Amboise, which overlooks the Loire River and was once home to kings and dreamers. Stroll through the royal apartments, visit the Gothic chapel where Leonardo da Vinci is buried, and enjoy panoramic views of the river and city below. Just up the hill and behind the old village is the Clo Lucet Mansion, where da Vinci lived out his final years. Inside you'll find intricate models of his inventions, bringing the Renaissance genius to life.

Discover the wines of the Loire

The Loire Valley is known for its diverse wine offerings, from crisp whites to delicate reds. To get to know the region's viticulture, visit a local winery near Vouvray or Chinon, where family-run wineries offer tastings and tours. Here you can taste the famous Loire Chenin Blanc, earthy Cabernet Franc and refreshing Rosé, often accompanied by local cheeses and breads. It's a truly fascinating way to get in touch with the region's rich farming traditions and taste the Loire Valley terroir.

Local tips for exploring the Loire Valley

  • The best time to visit:

    In spring and autumn, the weather is pleasant and there are fewer crowds, and in summer the region comes alive with festivals and open-air concerts.

  • Transportation:

    Renting a bike is a great way to explore the scenic Loire à Vélo cycling routes that connect many of the region's top attractions.

  • A hidden gem:

    Don't miss the little-known Chateau de Villandry, known for its Renaissance gardens. Spend a carefree day strolling through the maze of perfectly manicured flower beds, orchards and elegant vegetable gardens.
